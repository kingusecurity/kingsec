1 Executive Summary

Needs attention — no critical issues
A small number of items warrant attention; one should be addressed within the week.

This report summarizes an automated security assessment of acme.com, conducted on 05 July 2026. Overall, the site is in reasonable shape: we identified no critical vulnerabilities and no evidence of compromise. We did identify three issues worth addressing, along with four minor, non-urgent notes.

The most significant finding is a publicly visible, outdated software version, which makes the site an easier target for automated attacks. Alongside this, a set of protective browser settings are missing and an outdated encryption method remains enabled. Addressing the three priorities below would meaningfully reduce exposure with modest effort — two of the three can be handled in under an hour.

Recommended next step
Complete the three priority recommendations within the next seven days, then run another assessment to confirm they've been resolved.

2 Business Risk

In plain terms, none of the identified issues indicate that acme.com has been breached, and none expose customer data directly today. The risk is increased likelihood, not active loss: an exposed software version and missing protections make the site a more attractive, easier target over time — particularly to the automated scanning that opportunistic attackers rely on.

Left unaddressed, the practical exposures are reputational (a defaced or compromised site erodes confidence), continuity (recovery costs time and money), and trust (browser warnings from outdated encryption can deter visitors). These are manageable and, at present, low-to-moderate. The recommended actions are intended to keep them that way.

3 Top Priorities

Prioritized by potential business impact, likelihood of exploitation, and ease of remediation.

1
Missing security headers
Missing protective settings that help browsers block common attacks.
≈ 15 minutes
Recommended: Have your developer or web host add Content-Security-Policy, X-Frame-Options and Strict-Transport-Security (HSTS). Exact values are in the appendix.
2
Outdated encryption is still allowed
The site accepts an older, less-secure encryption protocol that modern browsers are phasing out.
≈ 1 hour
Recommended: Ask your hosting provider to disable the deprecated protocol (TLS 1.0/1.1) and keep only TLS 1.2 and 1.3.
3
An old software version is publicly visible
Attackers can see the version and look up its known weaknesses. Best handled by a professional.
≈ Half day
Recommended: Update to the current supported version, ideally with a professional to avoid downtime during the upgrade.

5 Quick Action Checklist

A simple checklist for whoever implements the fixes. Tick each item as it's completed, then re-run the assessment to confirm.

Enable HSTS
Add the Strict-Transport-Security header so browsers always use a secure connection.
Update TLS configuration
Ask your host to disable the outdated protocol (TLS 1.0/1.1) and keep only TLS 1.2 / 1.3.
Remove unnecessary server banner
Hide the software name and version from public responses so it isn't advertised to attackers.
Re-run assessment
Run KingSec again to confirm the fixes worked and your status has improved.
