TASK 1 — THE LIMITATIONS SECTION
================================================================================

CORRECTION TO THE BRIEF, stated plainly: the brief names seven
disclosures. Reading _limitations() in full, there are actually EIGHT
compressed into that one paragraph — the brief's seven plus severity
demotion (_severity_demotion_note: discloses when the content-based
classifier reduced a finding's severity below what the path/title alone
would suggest, and why). It's conditional — only renders when at least
one finding was demoted — which is probably why it read as absent from
the list. It survives in every design below like the other seven; noting
the count discrepancy now rather than silently matching "seven" to avoid
under-designing for it.

PROPOSAL: two tiers, not one restructure.

--- Tier 1 — "Scope at a Glance" (NEW, placed on page 2, immediately
    after the Executive Summary) ---

Three items only — the ones that bound WHAT was tested, which is exactly
what a reader who stops after page 2 needs to know before trusting
anything above it:
  - Authentication scope (unauthenticated only — full sentence, this is
    the one item that most changes what a reader thinks they're getting)
  - Port coverage (nmap's actual scanned range, in one sentence)
  - Scanner coverage (which scanners ran / didn't — a compact version;
    the existing "Scanner Coverage" section, a few sections later, stays
    as the detailed version with per-scanner reasons)

This is a genuine ADDITION, not a shortened substitute — the full-length
versions of all three stay in Tier 2 below, word-for-word. A short
front-page pointer and a full back-of-report disclosure are different
things serving different readers; this satisfies "every disclosure
survives" by construction, since nothing here replaces anything.

--- Tier 2 — "Limitations & Methodology Notes" (restructured, stays near
    the end, now grouped and subheaded instead of one paragraph) ---

Same eight disclosures, same words (verbatim reuse of the existing
derivation functions — _port_coverage_note, _rate_limit_note,
_severity_demotion_note, _authentication_scope_note, the CVE-note
branch, the opening two sentences, and the trailing config-change
sentence), regrouped under two subheadings rather than run together:

  "What This Assessment Did Not Cover" (scope group)
    - Authentication scope (full sentence)
    - Port coverage (full sentence, embeds the real recorded spec)
    - Rate limiting (full sentence, per-scanner detail)

  "Confidence and Methodology" (confidence group)
    - Point-in-time snapshot
    - False positive / false negative risk
    - CVE/CVSS correlation (the conditional branch, unchanged)
    - Severity adjustments (the demotion note, when present)
    - Configuration changes invalidate these results

Each becomes its own <h4> with 1-3 sentences under it, not a table —
a table reads well for terse, uniform rows; these vary from one
sentence (config-change) to a multi-clause list (severity demotion's
reason breakdown), and forcing that into table cells would either
truncate the longer ones or leave the table absurdly tall. Subheadings
scan properly at both lengths and both personas: the SME can skim eight
bold lines in ten seconds; the reviewer can read the sentence under
each without hunting through prose for where one disclosure ends and
the next begins.

Grouping rationale matches your own instinct in the backlog note
("possibly grouped by theme — scope/coverage vs. methodology/
confidence") — implemented as written.

TASK 2 — THE FOURFOLD REPETITION
================================================================================

Every one of the 45 findings currently renders in Risk Prioritization,
Findings (table), Technical Findings (full card), AND Remediation Steps
(full card) — two of those four are genuinely full-content renderings of
the same material (Technical Findings' facts/description/evidence, and
Remediation Steps' recommendation list), not just references.

Reader test, applied to each of the four:

  Risk Prioritization (ordered list, badge+title only)
    READER: the SME or their contractor doing a first pass — "what do I
    fix first." A ranked checklist, nothing more. KEEP, unchanged in
    content, but each item becomes a same-document anchor link straight
    to that finding's full card (WeasyPrint renders internal #anchor
    links as real clickable links in the PDF) — today it's a dead-end
    list; linking it turns "render #1 of 4" into "index #1 of 2."

  Findings (table: severity/title/status/evidence-count/rec-count)
    READER: the technical reviewer triaging or importing into a tracker
    — wants a scannable, spreadsheet-shaped overview before reading
    anything in full. KEEP, unchanged in content, same anchor-link
    treatment as above — "index #2 of 2."

  Technical Findings (full card: facts table, description, evidence)
    READER: the reviewer who wants the complete technical record.
    MERGE remediation into this card (see below) — this becomes the
    ONE place each finding's full content lives.

  Remediation Steps (full card: recommendation list)
    READER, as currently structured: nobody distinct. Whoever wants the
    fix for a finding is, by definition, also the person who just read
    (or is about to read) that finding's technical detail — today they
    have to leave the technical card, find the SAME finding again in a
    completely separate section, and read the fix in isolation from the
    evidence that justifies it. That's not a second audience, it's the
    same reader making two trips. MERGE into Technical Findings: each
    finding's full card gets a final "Remediation" subsection appended,
    reusing effective_recommendations() exactly as today — same content,
    zero information lost, one trip instead of two.

Net effect: 4 renderings of every finding become 1 full render (renamed
"Findings" — technical detail + remediation together) plus 2 compact,
genuinely-distinct indices that jump straight to it. For 45 findings
this does not just trim pages, it removes an entire category of
findings appearing 4 times each — the real bloat source named in the
brief.

TASK 3 — GROUP REPEATED FINDINGS
================================================================================

Checked this against the real baseline instead of reasoning abstractly.
The 45-finding assessment has not one but TWO strong grouping
candidates:

  - 10 "Open port N/tcp" findings (135, 902, 912, 3000, 3389, 5357,
    18080 at LOW; 445, 1001, 5678 at INFORMATIONAL) — the brief's own
    example, and the real count in this baseline happens to be exactly
    10, matching "10 network services exposed" precisely.
  - 10 "HTTP Missing Security Headers" findings, all INFORMATIONAL,
    identical title, presumably one per crawled path — an equally
    strong case the brief didn't name but the data makes obvious.

Together, 20 of 45 findings (44%) in this one real report are exact-
title-repeat noise from two finding families. That is the actual scale
of what grouping would fix here, not a hypothetical.

PROPOSED MECHANISM (presentation-layer only, as instructed):

Group findings that share ALL of: (a) the same title-prefix pattern
(e.g. "Open port ", or exact-title match for a family like "HTTP
Missing Security Headers" that has no natural prefix/suffix split), (b)
identical severity, (c) identical effective_recommendations (so two
findings that happen to share a title but got different remediation
text for some reason never merge). This runs entirely inside the
template layer, over report.entries, after every domain-layer
computation (severity_counts, executive_score, verdict) has already
finished reading the real ungrouped entries — grouping never touches
those inputs.

Rendered group: one card, title becomes "N network services exposed"
(or a comparable generated summary for other families — "N pages
missing security headers"), containing a compact table (e.g. Port |
Evidence summary) with each original finding's own evidence still
present — either inline per row (nmap's evidence is short: a banner
line) or as a per-row expand if a family's evidence runs long. No
evidence is dropped, summarized-away, or replaced by a synthetic
description — every individual finding_id, its full evidence, and its
own place in the immutable Report.entries snapshot stays exactly as
persisted; grouping is purely how those entries get laid out on the
page.

EXPLICIT ANSWER, as required before this touches anything scoring-
adjacent:

  Does grouping change the severity distribution chart? NO.
  Does grouping change report.total_findings or severity_counts? NO.

Both are computed in domain/report.py from the real, ungrouped
findings tuple, before rendering ever begins — grouping is a rendering
decision applied afterward, over a copy of entries used only to decide
how many <div> blocks to emit. The 45-finding count, the 1/0/5/11/28
severity breakdown, and the executive score all stay exactly what
they are today, undiminished by how the page groups them visually.
This is presentation, not a scoring interaction — but flagging it
explicitly, as instructed, rather than asserting it without saying so
plainly enough to be checked.

One open design question worth a decision before Step 2: does a GROUP
still get its own row in Risk Prioritization / the Findings table (as
one line, "10 network services exposed"), or does grouping apply only
inside the full-card section from Task 2? I'd propose: group
consistently across all three surviving views (the two indices AND the
full card) — an index that still lists 10 separate "Open port" lines
while the detail page shows 1 grouped card would read as inconsistent
and undercut exactly the "professional, not machine output" goal this
task exists for.

TASK 4 — THE EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
================================================================================

Current order: opening paragraph (target/date/verdict headline/count/
highest severity) → urgent note (Critical/High callout, when present) →
score panel (gauge) → action callout (separate paragraph, after the
gauge).

The brief's instruction ("three sentences... the number comes after")
is close to what's there today in raw HTML order, but the ACTION
sentence — "what to do first" — currently lives in a separate callout
AFTER the gauge, not folded into the opening three sentences. A reader
who reads the opening paragraph and stops sees WHAT was found but not
YET what to do about it; that only arrives after the visually-dominant
gauge, which is what makes the gauge read as the opening move even
though it isn't literally first in the markup.

PROPOSAL: three sentences, restructured to genuinely contain all three
required elements before the gauge:

  1. What was looked at — target, date, (cross-reference the new Scope-
     at-a-Glance block from Task 1 rather than repeat it)
  2. What was found — verdict headline, finding count, highest severity
     (unchanged content, already exists)
  3. What to do first — the SHORT version of today's late action
     callout, moved up here. For a Critical/High report this sentence
     becomes redundant with the urgent note that immediately follows it
     (both would say "act now") — so state it only when urgent_note
     will NOT also fire, exactly the same suppression logic
     _executive_summary already uses today for the existing action
     callout (Phase 2C Step 2 FIX 4's "the second dilutes the first"
     lesson, applied one line earlier instead of removed).

Then: urgent_note (Critical/High callout) — UNCHANGED POSITION, still
immediately after the three-sentence opening, still above the score
panel, still first and unconditional the way Phase 2C Step 1 built it
to be. This keeps its precedence exactly as instructed.

Then: the score panel (gauge + band + score narrative) — now genuinely
last, after every plain-language element, matching "the number comes
after."

The existing late action callout is then REMOVED, not duplicated — its
content is now sentence 3 above; leaving both would reproduce the
FIX-4 dilution defect this same file already fixed once.

Confirmed missing entirely — no section anywhere states what each

PROPOSAL: a new "Methodology" section, placed early — directly after
Scope at a Glance (Task 1) / the rewritten Executive Summary (Task 4),
since the reviewer persona's "how did you determine this" question
arrives as soon as they've read the verdict, not on page 20.

Content, entirely reusing existing derived data (no new domain logic
for the coverage facts — only one small, scoped addition, noted below):

  - Which scanners ran and their outcome: reuse Scanner Coverage's own
    grouping logic (same source data, same "Nmap, Nuclei: completed, 12

    factual backbone, with the existing Scanner Coverage section either

    Step-2 decision, not a Step-1 one — both are cheap; I lean toward
    folding it in, since a reviewer asking "how did you determine this"

  - One plain-language line per scanner on what it does — NEW static
    content, factual and well-known (same standard already applied to
    _GENERIC_REMEDIATION_BY_CWE's "real CWE-class guidance, not a
    fabrication"): e.g. "Nmap — discovers open network ports and
    running services." "Nuclei — tests for known vulnerability patterns
    using community-maintained templates." One sentence each for the
    six wired scanners (Nmap, Nuclei, Nikto, FFUF, Gobuster, ZAP).
  - What was and wasn't covered: reuse the SAME port-coverage/
    authentication-scope/rate-limit derivation functions Tasks 1 and 4
    already call — never a second, independently-worded copy that could
    drift from the Limitations section's version of the same fact.
  - What the operator configured: the assessment profile used (e.g.
    "Web Application Scan"). GAP FOUND WHILE INVESTIGATING: Assessment
    already carries a real profile_id property, but
    Report.from_assessment() never copies it into the Report dataclass
    — Report has no profile field today. This is a small, precise
    addition (one new Report field, one line in from_assessment()) that
    Methodology depends on; flagging it now so it's visible before
    approval, not discovered mid-implementation. No other data gaps

TASK 7 — ASSET ATTRIBUTION: FLAG, DO NOT FIX
================================================================================

Confirmed, and worse in the real baseline than the brief's example
alone suggests. _technical_finding_card's facts table hardcodes
("Affected Asset", target) — target is report.target, the assessment's
single URL/IP/hostname, applied identically to EVERY finding regardless
of what actually produced it. There is no per-finding asset field
anywhere in FindingSummary or the underlying Finding domain model to
draw from instead — this is a real, structural data gap, not a template
oversight, and it can't be fixed inside this phase's scope (report
templates only, no data-model changes) without stepping on the backlog
item already logged for it.

WHERE THIS SHOWS, concretely, in the real 45-finding baseline: all 10
"Open port N/tcp" findings — including port 3389 (RDP) and port 445
(SMB), both host-level services on whatever machine is listening at
127.0.0.1, not application-layer facts about the DVWA URL — will each
render "Affected Asset: http://127.0.0.1:18080" in their technical
card. A reviewer who knows what RDP and SMB are will read "open RDP
port" attributed to a URL and immediately flag it as an error in the
report, not a nuance — exactly the credibility risk the brief
describes.

HOW BAD IT LOOKS ONCE THE REST IS POLISHED: worse, not better. Today
the report reads as somewhat mechanical throughout (four repeated
renderings, a wall-of-text Limitations paragraph, ten identical "Open
port" entries) — a wrong asset attribution is one odd line among many
imperfections, easy to miss. After Tasks 1-4 land, the report will read
as considerably more professional and deliberate everywhere else; a
factually wrong "Affected Asset" field on a clean, well-organized page
will stand out MORE, not less, precisely because everything around it
now looks careful. This is the argument for promoting it after this
phase, stated as requested rather than left implicit.

No fix proposed or attempted here, per instruction.
